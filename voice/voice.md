# Patrick's LinkedIn voice

The purpose of this doc: give Claude (and future-Patrick) a reference for Patrick's register so that (a) surfaced news items can be framed in his voice, (b) content ideas can be scored for fit, and (c) any drafting help feels like Patrick wrote it.

Companion files:
- `pov.md` — Patrick's positions on recurring topics
- `writers.md` — style exemplars + anti-exemplar
- `examples/best/` — 8 canonical posts (primary source of truth for style)

**Note:** Patrick writes his own posts. This doc is for an *assistant*, not an automation. The bar for voice fidelity is high — not-quite-right is worse than no draft.

## Positioning in one sentence

Patrick is a technical, opinionated, community-embedded GTM engineer — ex-Clay, now running The Kiln — who writes practical posts with specific takes, specific tools, and specific people named.

## Voice fingerprint

Five traits that, taken together, identify a post as Patrick's:

1. **Claim-first, then evidence.** Hook states a specific observation or claim with a colon — no throat-clearing. "Clay just launched the feature that might get it from $3B → $10B." "Claude Code enrichment has gotten scarily good."

2. **Opinions are marked.** "I personally think…", "My personal opinion?", "I may be biased, but…". Patrick owns his takes visibly. Never hides behind passive voice or "some might say."

3. **Specificity everywhere.** Tool names, dollar amounts, time windows, named people. Not "a data provider" — "BlitzAPI at $500/mo." Not "some people I respect" — "Eric Nowoslawski, Brandon Charleson, Matthew Lucero." If a claim could be made with or without a specific example, Patrick uses the example.

4. **Self-aware humor, never snark.** "You might be thinking 'Patrick, this is so obvious,' and you'd be right." "Bribe the judges. (This is a joke Munnawar Hashim and Owen Ciha please have me back)." Warm, slightly self-deprecating, never punching down.

5. **Community-first.** Posts almost always name a collaborator, a teammate, or an admired peer. Shouting someone out is a default move, not a bonus. "Jesse Aseoff just made one of the coolest Clay tables…" "I credit a lot of my Clay learning to the early days when I was working there; Matt Maiale had me…"

## Structural moves

### Opener patterns (use one)
- **Claim + colon + promise:** "X has gotten scarily good. Here are a few learnings…"
- **Specific event + colon:** "Clay just launched the feature that might get it from $3B → $10B."
- **Shout-out hook:** "[Person] just made one of the coolest [things] I've seen in a while."
- **Context + list promise:** "We've hired a lot of GTM Engineers at The Kiln. Here are 3 great personas…"
- **Observation across clients:** "Here are a few GTM trends that I've noticed across our clients over the past few months:"

### Body pattern
Numbered list, 3–4 items (occasionally 5–7 for resource-style posts). Each item:
- **Bold one-line claim** (sentence case, ends with a period)
- 3–5 sentences of reasoning grounded in a specific tool, price, person, or example
- Sometimes a sub-structure (Why / When / What to watch out for) for personas/framework posts

### Closing pattern (use one)
- "Hope this is helpful!" + invitation + ⬇️
- "Interested to hear other thoughts / trends people have seen"
- "Let me know if I missed any good ones ⬇️"
- Optional P.S. for tangential ask (partnerships, DMs, product mentions)

### Signature moves
- ⬇️ to point to the first comment (where links live)
- P.S. for secondary CTAs ("DM me if you're building in X")
- Parenthetical asides for self-aware moments or inside jokes
- Em-dashes after list headers "1. The thing - explanation"

## Vocabulary

### Use
- **cracked** (as in "cracked GTME")
- **scarily good**, **absolutely nuts**, **mind-blowing**
- **massive**, **huge**
- **super** as intensifier (super technical, super specific, super direct)
- **actually** (as a nuance marker — "I actually think it could improve it")
- **the juice**, **the alpha** (for hard-won insight)
- **niche** (positive — niche signals, not broad)
- **at your fingertips**

### Avoid
- **leverage**, **synergy**, **unlock**, **enable** (generic B2B mush)
- **revolutionize**, **game-changer**, **disrupt** (overclaims)
- **journey**, **ecosystem** used loosely
- **stakeholders** (too corporate)
- **in today's fast-paced environment** or any time-context cliché
- LinkedIn-isms: "agree or disagree?", "thoughts?", "repost if you agree", bait-y engagement prompts
- Rhetorical question openers ("Ever wonder why…")

### Emoji use
Sparingly, functionally. Mostly terminal/role-related: 🦾 (cyborg — used for Eric), 🥾 (bootcamp — for Nathan), 🤵 (recruiting), 🏆 (winning), ⚡ (fast), ⬇️ (pointing to comment). Never decorative rows of mixed emojis in body copy.

## Typography quirks
- Sentence case everywhere. No Title Case or ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- Em-dashes (—) and double-hyphens (--) both appear. Not precious about consistency.
- Contractions always ("it's," "we've," "don't").
- Paragraph breaks are generous — most paragraphs are 1–4 sentences. Rarely denser.

## Drafting checklist (for Claude, before returning a draft)

1. Hook is claim-first, no throat-clearing, ends with a colon.
2. At least one specific tool/price/person named in the body.
3. At least one opinion is marked ("I personally think" or similar).
4. At least one shout-out to a collaborator, peer, or team member if natural.
5. No banned vocabulary (leverage, synergy, unlock, game-changer, etc.).
6. Sentence case. Paragraph breaks generous. Contractions on.
7. Close has an invitation, not a demand. ⬇️ if referencing comments.
8. If the post could be written by any GTM thought-leader, rewrite. Patrick's posts are unmistakably his.
